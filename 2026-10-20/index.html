The capital stack and DSCR

This is NESO, in brief. A capital stack is every source of money in a project, from senior debt to equity, in the order each gets repaid.

When the sources do not add up to the cost, the shortfall is called the gap. DSCR, the debt service coverage ratio, divides a building's net operating income by its loan payments.

Lenders use it to ask whether the building can carry its debt. CapStack puts senior debt, tax credits, grants, soft money and equity in one stack, and shows the gap and the order to secure each source. It is free, and Pro is $39.

ProForma models net operating income, debt service coverage, yield on cost and a ten-year cash flow, to answer whether a building pays back its loan. It is free, and Pro is $39.

You can read more at nesohq.com.

Reading a parcel before you commit

Here is a look at Bird's Eye. An entitlement path is the list of approvals a project needs, and the order they come in.

Knowing it before you go under contract is the point. An overlay district adds its own conditions on top of a parcel's base zoning, so two parcels with the same zoning can face different rules.

Three environmental red flags worth checking early on any site are wetlands, floodplain, and being next to a historic property. The Zoning and Entitlement Readiness report gives the parcel's zoning designation and permitted uses, overlay districts, the entitlement path — which approvals, in what order — a public-hearing and timeline risk read, and jurisdiction contacts. It is delivered in 2 to 3 business days.
